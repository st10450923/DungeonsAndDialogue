# Feedback Summary, Dungeons and Dialogue

**Event:** Makers Massive  
**Submitted by:** Ember Willow Jones, St10450923

\---

## Overview

Feedback was gathered from three attendees at Makers Massive, a game development and creative technology meetup. The game was presented as an interactive dungeon exploration experience in which the player navigates through rooms, inspecting environmental clues and using them as evidence in persuasion-based dialogue with NPC guards powered by a locally hosted LLM (Ollama). The following is a consolidated summary of the feedback received across all three conversations.

\---

## Full Feedback List

1. The art style needed more polish - it felt unfinished compared to the strength of the core concept.
2. The game would benefit from ambient sound and background music to improve immersion.
3. A voiceover system for NPC dialogue was suggested as a way to deepen the conversational feel of the game.
4. Voice input (speech-to-text) was proposed as an enhancement to make the player interaction feel more natural and conversational.
5. Keyboard input shortcuts for the UI were missing - specifically the ability to press Enter to submit text and Escape to close popups.
6. There was no restart button to return to the main menu from within the game.
7. The game over button did not function correctly - it did not close the game as expected.
8. NPCs felt repetitive across playthroughs due to fixed personalities; randomised NPC personalities were suggested to improve replayability.

\---

## Recurring Themes

### Immersion and Presentation

Three distinct areas of immersion came up across multiple conversations: visual polish, audio, and voice. Attendees consistently felt that the underlying concept, using gathered evidence to persuade an LLM-driven guard, was compelling, but that the presentation layer needed work to match that ambition. The absence of sound and music was the most frequently mentioned gap, raised by two attendees independently.

### Quality of Life and UX

Basic UI conveniences were noted as missing. Keyboard shortcuts (Enter to submit, Escape to dismiss popups), a functional quit button, and a main menu return option are standard expectations in game builds shown publicly. Their absence made the game feel like a work-in-progress rather than a polished prototype.

### Replayability

One attendee specifically raised the issue of NPC personality consistency across runs. Because the LLM prompts were fixed, guards responded similarly in tone and reasoning each playthrough. Randomised or varied personality archetypes were suggested as a relatively low-effort way to significantly increase replayability.

\---

## Initial Reactions

Presenting the game publicly was an informative experience. The core mechanic, evidence-based persuasion with a local LLM, drew genuine curiosity from attendees, particularly those with a technical background. The concept was understood quickly, which validated the clarity of the game's premise.

The feedback was largely constructive and focused on refinement rather than fundamental redesign, which was reassuring. The areas flagged(art, audio, UX polish, replayability) are all surface or systems-level improvements that do not require rethinking the core loop. The most unexpected piece of feedback was the suggestion of voice input, which had not been considered as a near-term feature, but makes conceptual sense for a dialogue-driven game.

\---

## Summary

The event confirmed that the game's core design is sound and communicates well to a mixed audience of developers and creatives. The primary feedback clusters around production polish, UI completeness, and replayability — all of which are addressable within the current project scope.
